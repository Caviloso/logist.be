A community for an industry that barely existed

Building Belgium’s drone community at EUKA, and finding it some heroes.

Case file no. 5
EUKA - Enabling the Drone Industry
Community & Innovation Manager
January 2017 – February 2019

FADE IN:

EXT. Sint-Truiden – 2017

Fruit trees as far as you can see. Above them, more and more often, a drone.

Kevin (V.O.)

In 2017, Belgium’s drone industry was young, promising and scattered. Start-ups in one corner, researchers in another, governments still working out the rules, and the people who could use drones mostly knew them from YouTube.

EUKA is a non-profit set up to grow a European drone and robotics industry. My job there was to turn all those corners into one community.

INT. A conference hall – daynight

A drone start-up, a fruit grower, a construction manager and a police officer stand around the same coffee table. None of them has met before.

Kevin (V.O.)

That coffee table was the whole idea.

INT. The EUKA office – the next two years

The approach.

  1. Give the community a place to find each other. I launched and ran the websites and social channels for EUKA and the Drone Community. Before people meet, they need somewhere to look each other up.

  2. Organise around their problems, not our technology. The events were for construction, agriculture, logistics and the police. A fruit grower doesn’t travel to an event about drones. They travel to an event about their orchard, and discover the drone there.

  3. Find the industry some heroes. In 2017 I organised Drone Hero Europe, EUKA’s contest with Commercial UAV Expo Europe in Brussels. Thirty-one entries from thirteen countries, an international jury and five “European Drone Heroes”, who got their prizes from Defence Minister Steven Vandeput and a trip to a drone convention in Las Vegas. A young industry needs stories before it has a market.

  4. Turn contacts into projects. I matched companies, knowledge institutions and governments on innovation projects, and wrote the grant applications to go with them, for VLAIO, EFRO, ESF and the European Commission. A grant application is storytelling with footnotes and a budget.

INT. The same conference hall – a year later

The coffee table is crowded. Nobody needs an introduction any more.

Kevin

(the lesson)

A community isn’t a mailing list. It’s a reason to show up. Give people a problem they care about and a few heroes to cheer for, and the networking takes care of itself.
