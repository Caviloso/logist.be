Marketing that sales actually uses

Mapping the B2B customer journey, from first contact to invoice.

Case file no. 2
Akkodis
Director of Marketing & Communications Benelux
November 2021 – Present

FADE IN:

INT. Any B2B company – Monday morning

Marketing presents a campaign. The slides are lovely. Sales nods politely and checks their phones under the table.

Kevin (V.O.)

Marketing and sales have a famous complaint about each other. Marketing says sales never follows up the leads. Sales says the leads are rubbish. In my experience, both are a bit right, and both are looking at a different stretch of the same road.

EXT. The client’s road – from first contact to invoice

A long road with a lot of signposts. Every department owns one stretch. Nobody owns the whole thing, except the client, who has to drive all of it.

Kevin (V.O.)

Marketing tends to see the road up to the lead. Sales sees it up to the signature. Delivery sees it from the first day of the project. Finance sees the invoice. The client sees all of it, in one go, and judges the brand on the worst bit.

INT. A meeting room with a very long whiteboard – daynight

The approach.

  1. Get the right people in the room. We mapped the journey together with sales and delivery, not about them. You can’t map a road if you’ve only ever driven the first kilometre.

  2. Sit in the client’s chair. Every step, from the first time a client hears the name to the moment the invoice lands, including the unglamorous bits in the middle. Especially those, actually.

  3. Mark where it gets bumpy. The moments where a client waits, doubts or has to chase us for an answer. Those are the places where marketing, sales and delivery can help each other, and where a nice campaign on its own changes nothing.

  4. Give every piece of marketing a stop on the road. Once the journey is on the wall, every event, every piece of content and every campaign has to answer one question: which step does this help? If nobody can point to one, it probably isn’t marketing that sales will use.

INT. The sales meeting – when it works

Marketing isn’t presenting this time. Marketing is sitting at the table.

Kevin

(the lesson)

The brand isn’t the bit before the sale. It’s the whole road. The invoice is a brand touchpoint too. And marketing that sales actually uses starts with marketing sitting in the sales meeting, not presenting at it.
