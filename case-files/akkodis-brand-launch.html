One brand out of two

Launching Akkodis in the Benelux after the AKKA–Modis merger.

Case file no. 1
Akkodis
Director of Marketing & Communications Benelux
November 2021 – Present

FADE IN:

INT. Two companies – the morning after

Two logos. Two email signatures. Two ways of answering the phone. And one new name that nobody has said out loud yet.

Kevin (V.O.)

In July 2021 the Adecco Group announced that it would bring together AKKA Technologies, known for its engineering, and Modis, its tech and digital business. When the deal closed in February 2022, the combined company got its global name: Akkodis.

On paper, that’s a press release. In real life, it’s a few thousand questions, and most of them start with “So what do we say now?” My job was to answer them in Belgium and the Netherlands, inside the company and outside it.

INT. A blank page – daynight

KEVIN writes two lists. Who has to believe this brand? Who didn’t ask for it?

Kevin (V.O.)

It turns out to be the same list. People from two companies, each with a history and a logo they were quite fond of. Clients who mainly want to know what changes for them, and what doesn’t. And engineers who might want to work with us, if we can explain what “us” means now.

Add two countries and three languages. Belgians and the Dutch share a language, more or less. They don’t share a way of saying yes.

INT. The writers’ room – the next months

The approach, in three acts.

  1. Positioning and messaging first. A global brand comes with a global story, and that’s a good thing. But a global positioning is like a script: every country still has to cast it. So the first job was to make the Akkodis story land in the Benelux, in Dutch, French and English, in words a Flemish engineer and a Dutch client would both use without wincing.

  2. Inside before outside. The internal rollout came before the external launch, on purpose. People inside a merger hear the news first, and they deserve to hear it properly, with room for questions. If your own colleagues can’t explain the new name at a birthday party, your clients won’t get it from a banner.

  3. Then the launch. Only once the story worked inside did it go outside, to clients, partners and the engineers we’d love to hire. Not as “look at our new logo”, but as an answer to the question every client asks after a merger: what does this mean for me?

INT. Reception – the day it really launches

Somebody answers the phone with the new name. Nobody checks the brand guide first.

Kevin

(the lesson)

A merger brand is a change project wearing a logo. The logo is the easy part. A new name doesn’t really launch on launch day. It launches the day your colleagues start using it in their own sentences.
