The magazine and the recycling bin

Six and a half years as editor-in-chief of Ondernemers, a magazine for entrepreneurs.

Case file no. 6
Voka - Chamber of Commerce & Industry Leuven
Editor-in-chief
July 2010 – December 2016

FADE IN:

INT. An office in Leuven – deadline day

Page proofs everywhere. A cold coffee. A printer that’s waiting for us. I read a headline out loud, cross it out in red pencil and write a better one above it.

Kevin (V.O.)

For six and a half years I was editor-in-chief of Ondernemers, the magazine of Voka – Chamber of Commerce & Industry Leuven. I ran it end to end, from the editorial planning to the day it went to print, and I wrote most of the feature articles myself.

INT. An entrepreneur’s desk – the morning the post arrives

Invoices, a brochure, two letters from the bank and our magazine. The recycling bin is right there.

Kevin (V.O.)

A member magazine has one real competitor, and it isn’t another magazine. It’s the recycling bin. Every issue lands on the desk of somebody who runs a business, between the invoices and the brochures, and gets about three seconds to earn a place on the coffee table instead.

INT. The editorial office – every issue

The approach.

  1. Put the entrepreneurs in front. People read about people. So the features were about the entrepreneurs themselves: how they started, what went wrong, what they’d do differently. The organisation’s own news had its place, but nobody keeps a magazine on the coffee table for the news about the organisation.

  2. Listen for the sentence they say off the record. The best quote in an interview usually comes when the founder forgets it’s an interview. Founders were nearly always better at explaining their company across a table than their own brochure was.

  3. Get colleagues writing. I got my colleagues writing about their own projects. They knew those stories better than I ever could. They just needed an editor, a word count and a deadline.

  4. Plan like a newsroom. An editorial plan, a fixed rhythm and a printer who doesn’t care how inspired you feel. A deadline is the best muse I know.

INT. A meeting room – today

A whiteboard, a team and a blank page. Some things never change.

Kevin

(the lesson)

Everyone has a story. Most people just need an editor and a deadline. I learned that at Ondernemers, and I still lead that way: the best stories come from getting colleagues to tell their own.

Insert – the public record

  • Voka still publishes its regional magazines under the name Ondernemers, for example Ondernemers Vlaams-Brabant (in Dutch).
