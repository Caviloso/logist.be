Choosing what matters

Three priorities, two countries, and the nerve to say no to the rest.

Case file no. 3
Akkodis
Director of Marketing & Communications Benelux
November 2021 – Present

FADE IN:

INT. A whiteboard – the start of the year

A long list of everything marketing could do this year. It goes on for a while. I uncap a marker and draw three boxes.

Kevin (V.O.)

Every marketing team has more ideas than hands. Ours did too: a small team, two countries, three languages, and a period when every euro had to work harder.

There are two ways to handle that. You can spread yourself thin and do everything a little. Or you can decide what the business needs most, and do that properly. I’m a fan of the second one.

Insert – the three boxes

  • A) Talent. Campaigns that get engineers to apply. An engineering company can only grow as fast as it finds engineers.
  • B) Sales. B2B events that put us in front of clients. Nothing beats a room with clients in it.
  • C) Brand. Content and awareness campaigns, including video, so people know the name before they need it.

INT. The same whiteboard – every week after

The approach.

  1. Put it on one page. Three priorities, one for each audience the business needed: engineers, clients and the market. Short enough to remember without looking it up.

  2. Test every request against the page. Does it bring in talent, put us in front of clients or build the brand? Then it’s in. If not, it waits. Most good ideas survive a wait. The others were never that good.

  3. Make work do two jobs. A good video about the work engineers really do speaks to clients and to future colleagues in the same ninety seconds. When you’re short of hands, that kind of double duty is gold.

  4. Say no out loud, with a reason. A quiet no turns into a rumour. A no with a reason turns into a strategy that the rest of the business can follow, and even use themselves.

INT. A planning meeting – when it works

Someone pitches a shiny new idea. Before I can say a word, the first question in the room is which box it goes in.

Kevin

(the lesson)

Michael Porter wrote it back in 1996: “The essence of strategy is choosing what not to do.” Every marketer nods at that. The hard part is doing it on a Tuesday, when the shiny idea comes from someone you like. A no with a reason is a strategy. A no without one is just a bad mood.

Insert – the public record

  • Michael E. Porter, “What Is Strategy?”, Harvard Business Review, November–December 1996.
  • No press release for this one either: it’s how the work got done. My role and dates are on LinkedIn.
