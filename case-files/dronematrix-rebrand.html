Making a drone company make sense

A new brand and a new website for DroneMatrix, and communication through COVID.

Case file no. 4
DroneMatrix
Marketing & Communications Manager Belgium
March 2019 – October 2021

FADE IN:

EXT. A field in Limburg – dawn

A white box stands at the edge of a site. Its roof slides open. A drone rises, flies its round, comes back and lands in the box. Nobody touched a joystick.

Kevin (V.O.)

That’s DroneMatrix. A Limburg company, founded in 2015, with its head office in Hasselt and its factory in Diepenbeek. It builds drone-in-a-box systems and offers them as a service: drones that fly automated missions from their own docking station.

I joined in 2019 as a freelancer, to lead marketing and communications in Belgium.

INT. The engineering floor – daynight

Brilliant people, explaining brilliant technology, in exactly the order they built it.

Kevin (V.O.)

That’s not a criticism. It happens in every good engineering company: the technology grows up faster than the brand. The people who built it know every component by heart, so that’s where the story starts.

But the person on the other side of the table doesn’t want a drone. They want to know what happened on their site last night.

INT. The writers’ room – daynight

The approach.

  1. Start from the client’s problem, not the spec sheet. What does the drone do for the person who’s responsible for a site? Answer that first. The specs are true, and they matter, but they’re chapter two.

  2. A brand as grown-up as the technology. The rebrand had one job: make a young company look like what it already was, a serious player in a serious market.

  3. A website for two kinds of reader. The new website had to tell a visitor in a few seconds what the drones do for them, and still give the engineers on the client side all the depth they came for. Story at the front, technical detail one click further.

INT. A kitchen table – spring 2020

A laptop, a video call and someone saying “I think you’re still on mute.” COVID has arrived.

Kevin (V.O.)

Then the world went home, and communication suddenly had a second job. When nobody’s in the same room, internal communication is the office. So alongside the rebrand, I ran internal and external communications through the COVID period: keeping colleagues in the loop, and keeping the outside world informed about a company that was still very much at work.

EXT. The same field – dusk

The box closes. The drone is home.

Kevin

(the lesson)

Explaining hard tech simply isn’t dumbing it down. It’s doing the hard work so your reader doesn’t have to.
